The scenario

Every ad waits in one inbox, and most come back at least once

Northstar Harbor Mortgage is a fictional lender. Its loan officers write their own social posts, emails and flyers. Every ad must pass a compliance review under Regulation Z before it runs. Ads arrive by email, reviewers check each one from scratch, and about half go back for fixes. Each round adds days.

~1,147ads submitted each month

These are planning assumptions for the fictional pilot, not measured results. The targets (20% returned, 10 minutes per ad) are what the rollout would measure against.

What I built, and where to find it

Loan officers run a Claude skill on each ad before submitting. They get a PASS, FLAG or HOLD scorecard, approve the fixes, and submit a cleaner ad with the report attached. Reviewers start from that report instead of a blank page. Around the skill, I built everything a real rollout needs.

Step 1: The intake form

Every request to AI Enablement starts with the same short Microsoft Form. This is what Marketing sent.

AI ENABLEMENT · INTAKE REQUEST REQ-2026-0091

Request help with an AI use case

Submitted by Jordan Ellis, Marketing Manager · Tue, Sep 8, 2026

1. What process do you want help with?
Getting loan officer ads approved by Compliance.
2. What's the problem today?
Approvals take a week or more. Loan officers complain constantly and some campaigns miss their window.
3. What would success look like?
AI reviews and approves ads automatically so loan officers can post the same day.
4. How often does this happen?
Over 1,000 ads a month.
5. What information is involved?
Ad drafts, flyers, social posts, emails. No customer info (I think).
6. Who else is involved?
Compliance reviewers, loan officers.
7. How urgent is this?
High. Spring campaigns start planning in January.
What I noticed before the meeting Answer 3 proposes a solution, not a goal. "Approves automatically" would remove the licensed reviewer. I needed to find the goal underneath it. Answer 5 is a guess. "I think" means nobody has checked whether customer data ends up in ads. Missing: why approvals take a week. The form says how long, not where the time goes. And Compliance, the team that owns the process, didn't write this request.

INSIGHT 1

The ask was AI approval. The need is speed.

Form: "AI reviews and approves ads automatically."

Reframed: an AI pre-screen before submission. A licensed reviewer still signs every ad.

Root cause: missing APRs, NMLS numbers and equal housing lines send ads back for rounds of rework.

Form: "Loan officers complain constantly."

Real risk: when waits are long, some loan officers post without approval. Faster review is a compliance control.

Who gets it and how

Single sign-on and group sync

Users sign in through Northstar's single sign-on (SSO), so access follows their Northstar account. SCIM provisioning keeps the Loan Officer, Marketing and Compliance groups in sync with the HR directory, so access changes automatically when someone joins, changes roles or leaves.

Packaged as a plugin

In production, the skill ships inside a plugin assigned to the Loan Officer, Marketing and Compliance groups. Loan officers find it pre-installed and never upload anything.

Gated by acknowledgment

Each user acknowledges the Data Rules card before their first live review. Responses go to the rollout dashboard, so admins can see who is cleared to use it.

Rule changes are controlled

Compliance owns the rule content and signs off every change. Changes ship once a month as a new skill version with release notes; urgent regulatory changes ship the same week.

Governance operations · 20%

Put the policy where the work happens

An AI Acceptable Use Policy only works if people can apply it in the moment. The Data Rules card turns it into five rules for this one tool, and every user signs off before their first live review.

Policy in practice

Data Rules for Ad Review

Governance
  1. Never paste customer information. The assistant stops and asks you to remove it.
  2. Know what's fine to use: your ad copy, your own NMLS ID and work contact details, sample numbers.
  3. Only use Northstar's Claude, never a personal account or another AI tool.
  4. A PASS is not approval. Publish only the exact version Compliance approves.
  5. Report mistakes right away. No blame.

Design choices

Usage by role, access by name

Usage is reported by role and branch, because using the tool is optional and the dashboard drives adoption, not surveillance. Access is reviewed by name, because you can't confirm the right people have access, or catch accounts that shouldn't, without seeing who they are.

Cost shown next to value

Seats are already licensed, so cost is estimated usage per ad. In March, about $261 in usage freed about $28,900 in reviewer capacity.

Every number names its source

Claude workspace usage, the SharePoint review list, Microsoft Forms or ServiceNow. Enablement owns the measures; Engineering owns the data plumbing.

Status on every measure

On target, near or short, with the change from last month. Feedback themes show what's fixed, planned or logged for the app.

A field test with a working loan officer

I ran the skill live with a licensed loan officer on a real co-marketing flyer for a home listed in Douglasville, Georgia. The skill was on, the flyer was attached, and there was no prompt. The skill returned a HOLD and seven action items. She approved every fix, and the revised mockup came back as a FLAG with only a few items left for a person to finish. Below the real test, an illustrative third round shows how the last items would be cleared.

Real flyer and real reports, shared with the loan officer's permission. To protect the people and companies involved, I blurred names, faces, phone numbers, emails, license and NMLS numbers, company names and logos, the QR code and the street address. In the reports, those details are replaced with labels like [Agent] and [Lender].

What the skill caught

  • HOLD Down payment percentages with no repayment terms or APR
  • FLAG Unclear company NMLS label; "Equal Housing Lender" missing
  • FLAG Spelling mismatches and a broken email address with a space in it
  • FLAG Tiny, letter-spaced, low-contrast fine print
  • FLAG A phone number that looked like a placeholder, and an unconfirmed QR destination

It also noted for the reviewer that a co-marketing piece needs a RESPA Section 8 cost-split check, plus Georgia's own ad rules.

What she valued

  • The report explained why each item was flagged, not just what
  • It caught empty placeholders and copy-paste errors a busy person skims past
  • She approved all the fixes and was happy with the mockup

Where it stopped, on purpose

The re-check left the "[GA license]" and "[apply URL]" placeholders and safer VA wording for a person to finish. Next step in production: the designer finalizes, and Compliance signs off.
