Northstar Harbor: Ad Review Workflow, Before and After

Northstar Harbor Mortgage is a fictional company created for portfolio purposes. Policies shown are illustrative.

Key: 🤖 Claude (with the mortgage-ad-review skill) · 📁 SharePoint review list · 💬 Teams · 📧 Email · 🎨 Design tool · 📣 Marketing team · ⚙️ Power Automate (the automation glue)

Before: email-driven review

Every ad travels by email. The loan officer guesses at the rules, the reviewer starts from a blank page, and every fix means a new attachment.

Bottlenecks in the before state

  • Loan officer: no way to check an ad before submitting, so most ads bounce back at least once.
  • Reviewer: an unsorted inbox, no context, and every ad reviewed line by line from zero.
  • Versions: each round creates a new attachment, so it's unclear which version was approved.
  • Publishing: loan officers post on their own, so nobody can confirm the approved version is the one that went live.
  • Audit trail: approvals are scattered across personal inboxes.

After: pre-screened, one queue, one record

The loan officer fixes problems before submitting, the reviewer gets one sorted queue with a scorecard on every item, and Marketing publishes only approved versions.

Accuracy check on product claims: this workflow assumes Northstar's org knowledge (current loan products, programs and approved product descriptions) is connected to Claude. Alongside the Regulation Z and house-standard checks, the skill compares each ad's product claims against that source and flags anything that doesn't match, such as a program Northstar doesn't offer or outdated terms. The reviewer still makes the final call.

What changes for each person

  • Loan officer: catches issues in minutes, before Compliance ever sees the ad, and gets comments in Teams instead of a long email thread.
  • Reviewer: one queue, sorted by urgency, with the scorecard as a head start. PASS items get a quick confirmation; time goes to the FLAG items that need judgment.
  • Marketing: publishes only items marked Approved, from one source of truth.
  • Compliance leadership: every ad has one record with its versions, reviewer, dates and the approved file.

Fictional Northstar policy

  1. Every ad gets a human compliance sign-off, including ads the skill marks PASS.
  2. Loan officers run the skill and attach its report to every submission.
  3. Returned ads are resubmitted as a new version on the same SharePoint item, never as a new email. The Submission ID links every version, report and comment to that one item, the way a ticket number does.
  4. Marketing publishes approved ads on company channels and sends marketing emails from the company's email marketing platform, signed by the loan officer, with replies going to the loan officer. Loan officers may publish approved ads on their own social accounts, using only the exact approved version, and must mark the item Published with the live link. Every queue item records who publishes it.
  5. Compliance signs off every change to the skill's rules before it ships at the monthly release. Decisions to move to the next phase go to the AI Governance Committee.

What gets built and by whom

  • Enablement lead builds: the skill, the SharePoint review list, the Teams alerts, the approval receipt email and the monthly report, using approved connectors and low-code tools.
  • Handed to engineering: any deeper system integrations, with a clear problem statement and requirements.

Future vision (outside this workflow)

A separate, supplementary tool could help loan officers create compliant, brand-aligned ads from the start, such as approved templates where only the rate and loan officer details change. Compliance and the AI Governance Committee would decide whether pre-approved templates need a lighter review. It is noted here as a next step, not part of this build.

Path to scale: from skill to engineered app

The tool grows only when the data shows it is worth it. Enablement proves the value with a lightweight build, then hands a clear problem statement and requirements to engineering for anything heavier. The gates below are illustrative targets for the fictional Northstar pilot.
