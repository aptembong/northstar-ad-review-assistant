The pre-screen goes first. You make the call.

Every ad in the Ad Review Queue has already been checked by the Ad Review Assistant. This guide covers how to work the queue, how far to trust the pre-screen, and how to help tune its rules.

You spend your time on judgment calls, not on finding a missing APR.

What the pre-screen checks, and what's still yours

The assistant is a first pass. It is thorough on rules that can be written down and checked against the text. It is weaker on context, timing and intent. That's where your review matters most.

IT CHECKS EVERY AD FOR
  • Rates and APR (Reg Z 1026.24(c)): any rate stated without an APR.
  • Triggering terms (1026.24(d), (f)): down payment, term, payment or finance charge without the required disclosures nearby.
  • Prohibited practices (1026.24(i)): misleading "fixed," implied government endorsement, "counselor," split-language disclosures.
  • Northstar house standards: NMLS IDs, Equal Housing Lender, as-of date, credit approval language, no pressure claims.
  • Product accuracy: product claims checked against Northstar's connected loan product knowledge.

A deterministic scan lists every candidate trigger word, and the report must address each one. Every finding quotes the ad and names its rule, so you can check it in seconds.

STILL NEEDS YOUR JUDGMENT
  • Benefit and savings claims that aren't triggering terms but can still mislead, such as "free up hundreds a month."
  • Timing: whether the rate and as-of date will still be true on the planned publish date.
  • Context outside the ad: the audience, the landing page, where it runs, and who publishes it.
  • Anything it flagged as unreadable: small image text and video frames it couldn't fully read.
  • The decision itself. The assistant never approves an ad. Only a licensed reviewer does.

What each verdict means by the time it reaches you

PASS
No issues found by the pre-screen. Still read the whole ad. PASS means "ready for your sign-off," never "approved."
FLAG
Arrives with open items. The loan officer skipped a fix or the assistant wants a human look. Start with the Open items list in the packet.
HOLD
Shouldn't reach you. The assistant won't offer submission at HOLD. If one arrives, choose Changes Requested and open a service desk ticket so AI Enablement can find out why.

3

Review what the pre-screen can't

The disclosures are in place, so spend your time on judgment. Ask four questions of every ad:

  • Would a customer take any promise here literally?
  • Will the rate and dates still be true on the publish date?
  • Does it fit the channel, audience and publisher?
  • Do I agree with every finding, and did it miss anything?

Decide: approve or request changes

Choose Changes Requested and add your comments, or choose Approved and sign the Compliance decision section of the packet. Please don't edit the ad yourself. Sending it back keeps the version history clean.

Write comments the assistant can act onOne change per comment. Quote the exact words. Say what you need, with example wording if you have it. The loan officer pastes your comments into a new chat, and the assistant turns each one into an action item.
DANA'S COMMENTS · STATUS: CHANGES REQUESTED

When a rule needs to change

Compliance owns the rules. AI Enablement owns the skill. Rule changes ship once a month, so everyone gets the same version at the same time.

1

Compliance files a request

Use the Ad Review rule change request form, pinned in the Compliance Teams channel. Include the rule and citation, example ad wording, and the verdict you expect.

2

Enablement updates and re-tests

AI Enablement updates the skill, then re-runs the full sample ad set and every past miss to check that nothing else changed.

3

Compliance signs off

The Compliance lead reviews the before-and-after test results and signs off. Nothing ships without that sign-off.

4

It ships at the monthly release

The new version goes out in the plugin for the Loan Officer, Marketing and Compliance groups. A release note posts in Teams, and the guides are updated the same day.

How Liam's email feeds the next release. Dana's "Missed an issue" rating joined two similar ratings that month. AI Enablement drafted a request with the Compliance lead: flag benefit and savings claims, and compare the as-of date with the planned publish date. After sign-off, it's planned for skill version 7. Urgent regulatory changes don't wait for the monthly release. They can ship the same week.

Quick answers

It arrived at PASS. Do I still read the whole ad?

Yes. PASS means the pre-screen found nothing. It doesn't replace your review.

The loan officer skipped a fix.

It's listed under Open items. You decide whether it stands. Skipping a fix is allowed. It isn't automatically wrong.

I see customer information in a submission.

Don't approve it. Choose Changes Requested, then open a service desk ticket marked "AI data incident." See the Data Rules card.

Can I fix one word myself?

Please don't. Send it back with the exact wording. A changed ad is a new version and needs a fresh pre-screen.

I disagree with a finding.

Your call stands. Approve it if it's fine, and rate the pre-screen "Flagged something that was fine" so the rule gets tuned.
