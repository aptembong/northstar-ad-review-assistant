BRANCH AI CHAMPION KIT

Be your branch's first stop for AI.

Branch AI champions are loan officers who help their branch use Northstar's AI tools well. You answer "how do I" questions, run hands-on labs when a tool rolls out, and bring your branch's feedback back to AI Enablement. The Ad Review Assistant is the first tool you'll support.

About 2 hours a month. You're not the rule expert, and you don't need to be.

The role

Champions make Northstar's AI tools easy to use, for every tool AI Enablement rolls out. The business owner of each tool owns its rules, and AI Enablement owns the tools. Keeping those lines clear is what makes the role light.

YOU DO
  • Answer "how do I…?" questions: finding a tool, writing a good prompt, reading the output, knowing what to do next.
  • Run the hands-on labs when a tool rolls out, and for new hires after that.
  • Watch your branch's numbers for each tool every month, and nudge people who aren't set up yet.
  • Bring feedback back: confusing moments, wrong answers and ideas for new tools go to AI Enablement each month.
YOU HAND OFF
  • Rule and policy questions go to the team that owns them, such as Compliance for ad rules. Never guess at a rule.
  • Anything broken goes to the service desk: a missing skill, an error, an alert that never came.
  • Customer data pasted by mistake goes to the service desk as an "AI data incident," copying Compliance.
  • Approving work. Champions never approve anything a tool produces. For ads, a compliance reviewer signs off on every one.
1 or 2champions per branch. Your branch manager nominates you; volunteers are welcome too. Branches with more than 75 loan officers have two.
~3 hoursto get ready, one time: the train-the-trainer session, prep, and co-leading one lab with AI Enablement. Each new tool adds about an hour.
~2 hoursa month after that: a 30-minute champions call, a lab when you have new hires, and a short feedback roundup.

Answering your branch's questions

The same four doors work for every tool. Most questions are about how to use it, and those are yours. When a question is about a rule, a broken tool or customer data, hand it off quickly. You don't need to solve it, just send it to the right place.

"HOW DO I…?"

You answer

Prompts, reports, fixes, resubmitting, what PASS means. The answer sheet covers the top questions.

"IS THIS BROKEN?"

Service desk ticket

Help them open it with the Submission ID and a screenshot. Then check back in a day.

"IS THIS COMPLIANT?"

The rule owner

For the Ad Review Assistant, that's Compliance on the Teams review thread for the Submission ID. Don't interpret the rule yourself.

"IT GOT THIS WRONG."

The tool's feedback form

They file it, and you mention the pattern in your monthly roundup.

Your monthly rhythm

Same four touchpoints every month. Together they take about 2 hours.

1

Champions call

30 minutes, first Tuesday of the month in Teams. What's shipping next across tools, branch questions, and one tip to take back.

2

Preview the release

Try each tool's next version a week before it ships, then share the release note with your branch on launch day.

3

Check your numbers

Readiness, usage and quality for each tool, filtered to your branch on the AI dashboard. Nudge anyone who isn't set up.

4

Send your roundup

Questions, confusing moments and wrong answers from your branch, one form per tool, starting with "Champion" and your branch.

When a new tool rolls out

Every tool AI Enablement ships comes with the same champion package, so supporting a second or third tool feels familiar.

EVERY NEW TOOL INCLUDES
  • A champion briefing on the monthly call, plus about an hour of tool-specific train-the-trainer.
  • A hands-on lab and facilitator guide, piloted by AI Enablement before champions run it.
  • An answer sheet with the top questions and who takes each one.
  • Branch numbers for readiness, usage and quality on the AI dashboard.
