LEADERSHIP BRIEFING · NOVEMBER 2026

Faster ad approvals. Compliance still makes every call.

The Ad Review Assistant pre-screens every loan officer ad before it reaches Compliance. Reviewers spend their time on judgment, not on finding missing disclosures. This briefing covers the problem, what changes, what it's worth, and four things we need from you.

Summary

Every ad a loan officer writes has to clear Regulation Z and our house standards. That review is careful work, and it has become a bottleneck.

THE PROBLEM

More ads than reviewer hours

About 1,147 ads a month from 2,032 loan officers. Six part-time reviewers have 408 hours for ads but need 757. Half of all ads go back at least once, and each round takes 3 to 5 business days. Loan officers wait, or they're tempted to skip review.

WHAT WE BUILT

A pre-screen before Compliance

Loan officers run each draft through the Ad Review Assistant in Northstar's Claude. It checks disclosures and house standards, offers fixes, and submits a clean packet to the Compliance queue in SharePoint, with a Teams alert. It never approves an ad.

WHAT CHANGES

Fewer rounds, faster decisions

Ads arrive with the routine problems already fixed. Reviewers spend about 10 minutes per ad instead of 22, fewer ads go back, and reviewers decide within 1 business day. Compliance keeps full sign-off and owns every rule.

~$30,600 / month

Reviewer capacity freed, illustrative. About 528 fewer reviewer hours a month (757 → ~229) at an assumed $58 an hour fully loaded, or about $367,000 a year. That time goes back to the reviewers' other Compliance work and closes the 349-hour shortfall without adding headcount.

How risk stays managed

Speed only matters if Compliance trusts the process. These controls are in place from day one.

A licensed reviewer signs off on every adThe assistant can't approve or publish. PASS means "ready for review," never "approved."
No customer dataEvery user acknowledges the Data Rules before access. If data is pasted by mistake, it's a P1 with Compliance and InfoSec within an hour, and no blame for reporting.
Compliance owns the rulesRule changes ship monthly, after re-testing and Compliance sign-off. Reviewers rate the pre-screen's accuracy on every decision.
One record per adEach ad has a locked queue record with every version, comment and decision, ready for an exam or audit.

What we need from you

Four asks. Two for executives, two for branch managers. Each one has a date.

Executives
1

Protect the time

Let each branch AI champion spend about 3 hours on setup, then about 2 hours a month. Give every loan officer 30 minutes for the hands-on lab, on the clock.

Confirm by December 4, 2026
2

Agree to the app decision gate

When the assistant meets its targets company-wide for 2 months in a row, AI Enablement and Engineering bring a build proposal for the Ad Review app to the AI Governance Committee. You agree now to review it then.

Earliest decision: June 2027 Committee meeting
Branch managers
3

Nominate your branch AI champion

One champion per branch, two if you have more than 75 loan officers. Volunteers are welcome. Champions get early access, visible credit, and a direct line to the rules.

Nominations due January 15, 2027
4

Own your branch's readiness

100% of loan officers acknowledge the Data Rules, and at least 80% complete the lab, before your branch goes live. Your champion brings you the numbers each month.

THE DECISION GATE

Build is proposed only when, company-wide, for 2 months in a row:

20% or fewer ads are returned
90% or more of submitted ads were pre-screened
No unresolved P1 data incidents

Then AI Enablement and Engineering bring a build proposal, with scope, cost and timeline, to the AI Governance Committee.
