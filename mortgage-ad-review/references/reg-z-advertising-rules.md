# Regulation Z advertising rules for mortgage ads

Source: 12 CFR 1026.24 (Truth in Lending Act, Regulation Z), as published by the CFPB at consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/24. This is a plain-language summary for pre-screening only. Compliance reviewers use the full regulation and its official commentary.

## 1026.24(b) Clear and conspicuous
All required disclosures must be clear and conspicuous. Fine print hidden at the bottom of an image, very small text, or text that flashes by in a video should be FLAGGED for review.

## 1026.24(c) Rates must be stated as an APR
- If the ad states a rate of finance charge, it must state it as an "annual percentage rate" using that term.
- If the APR can increase after the loan closes, the ad must say so.
- Mortgage (dwelling-secured) ads may also state a simple interest rate, but only alongside the APR (see 24(f)).

**Pre-screen check:** "Rates as low as 5.99%" with no APR → HOLD.

## 1026.24(d) Triggering terms
If an ad states ANY of these:
1. the amount or percentage of a down payment ("3% down", "$10,000 down")
2. the number of payments or period of repayment ("30-year loan", "360 payments")
3. the amount of any payment ("$1,450/month")
4. the amount of any finance charge ("closing costs of $2,000" when those are finance charges)

...then the ad must ALSO state:
- the down payment amount or percentage,
- the terms of repayment over the full term of the loan, and
- the APR, using that term, and whether it can increase.

**Not triggering terms:** general phrases such as "low down payment", "affordable monthly payments", "flexible terms", "no down payment options available" (be careful: "0% down" IS a triggering term because it states a percentage).

## 1026.24(f) Extra rules for dwelling-secured ads (not TV or radio)
- **Rates:** if the ad states a simple interest rate and more than one rate will apply over the loan (for example an ARM or an introductory rate), it must show each rate, how long each applies, and the APR, with equal prominence and close to the advertised rate.
- **Payments:** if the ad states a payment amount, it must show each payment that will apply over the term (including any balloon), how long each applies, and that the payment does not include taxes and insurance (if applicable) so the actual payment will be greater. This must have equal prominence and be close to the advertised payment.

## 1026.24(i) Prohibited practices for dwelling-secured ads
1. **"Fixed"**: using "fixed" for rates or payments in an adjustable-rate or payment-increasing product, unless the ad labels it "Adjustable-Rate Mortgage" (or "Variable-Rate Mortgage") with equal prominence, and each use of "fixed" states how long it is fixed and that it can change afterward.
2. **Misleading comparisons**: comparing a current or hypothetical payment or rate to one that applies only for part of the loan term, without the required context.
3. **Government endorsement**: calling the product a "government loan program" or "government-supported loan", or implying government sponsorship. Exception: actual FHA, VA, or similar government-backed programs may be named as such.
4. **Another lender's name**: using a consumer's current lender's name without clearly identifying the advertiser and stating that the ad is not from that lender.
5. **Debt elimination**: misleading claims that the loan will eliminate debt or waive existing obligations.
6. **"Counselor"**: calling a for-profit broker or lender a "counselor".
7. **Two languages**: giving some trigger terms or disclosures only in a foreign language and others only in English in the same ad.

## Other rules to mention, not decide
These may apply, but the pre-screener only flags them for the reviewer:
- the Mortgage Acts and Practices Advertising Rule (Regulation N, 12 CFR 1014), which bans misrepresentations in mortgage ads
- state advertising and licensing rules, which vary
- fair lending and fair housing rules, which bar discriminatory images or wording
